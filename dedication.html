Who we remember

The passengers and crew of RMS Titanic were largely recorded in official manifest lists and subsequent Board of Trade enquiries. Yet many among the steerage travellers, the galley hands, and the stokers died without a name preserved in any public register — lost to the sea before a survivor could speak for them. The boundary stones at Halifax's Fairview Lawn Cemetery and Southampton's Southern Cemetery carry those who have none.

The In Memoriam footer on each souvenir viewcard echoes that act of remembrance: twelve named characters appear on the card itself, but the count beneath honours fifteen hundred souls.

On the film

James Cameron's Titanic (1997) brought the disaster story to a new generation and seeded the character-named presets used in this studio — Jack, Rose, Ismay, Brady, and others drawn from the film's cast. This project does not re-enact the sinking or dramatise the tragedy; it offers a quiet craft activity through which anyone can assemble a personal memorial sheet.
